## 🗂️ Activity Breakdown

<details>
<summary><b>1️⃣ Presentation Design Principles — click to expand ⬇️</b></summary>
<br>

**What it is:** A promotional flyer for a free coffee booth event at Arrupe Hall, Booth 7 — offering iced latte, cold brew, and Spanish latte at no cost.

**Concept:** A simple, high-impact flyer meant to catch attention fast and clearly say "it's free."

**Design principles I applied:**
- 🎯 **Contrast** — bold text against a soft cream background so the main message stands out immediately
- 📐 **Visual hierarchy** — the booth number is largest/boldest (main message), the date is smallest (context)
- 🌬️ **Whitespace & grid alignment** — a subtle background grid and consistent margins keep it organized, not cluttered
- ☕ **Imagery as focal point** — a large coffee photo anchors the layout; food photography naturally draws the eye
- ✏️ **Supporting line-art** — a moka pot and coffee beans reinforce the theme without competing with the text

**Why it works:** scannable in seconds — a viewer instantly understands what's offered, where, when, and that it's free.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>2️⃣ Color Palette & Typography — click to expand ⬇️</b></summary>
<br>

**What it is:** A personal brand identity built around my initials, "SD" — logo, business card, and a color/type reference sheet.

**Why blue + off-white:** Blue conveys trust, professionalism, and calm — fitting for a personal academic brand. Off-white softens the palette so it doesn't feel harsh or overly corporate.

**Why Montserrat + Fira Code:** Montserrat is bold and geometric, great for headings that need visual impact. Fira Code is a monospace font with a clean, slightly technical feel — appropriate for an IT skills course and used for body text/details.

**Consistency:** the same palette and type pairing carries across every asset in this set, tying it together as one cohesive brand.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>3️⃣ Infographic & Mini Project Documentation — click to expand ⬇️</b></summary>
<br>

**What it is:** An infographic, *"Waiting for Care: Long Hospital Waiting Times,"* plus a full project proposal, *"Easing the Wait."*

**The problem:** Long waits happen at nearly every stage of a hospital visit — registration, diagnostics, consultation, pharmacy — largely due to manual, paper-based processes and high patient volume.

**Key data:** Average time spent by new patients in UP-PGH's outpatient department, from registration through consultation: **139.97 minutes (~2 hrs 20 min)**. WHO notes that delays specifically in emergency care can have serious consequences.

**Main causes:** high patient volume • limited healthcare staff • manual processes

**Design choices:** same brand palette for portfolio-wide consistency, icon-based sections (clock, hospital, queue) for quick scanning, and a clear four-part structure — Problem → Average Wait Time → Main Causes → Solution.

**Proposed solution — MediQueue:**
- 📲 Online booking portal + QR-code check-in
- ⚖️ Smart scheduling & load-balancing (prioritizes urgent cases)
- 🖥️ Centralized dashboard for staff
- 🔔 Real-time SMS/app notifications
- 📊 Analytics module tracking wait times & peak-hour patterns

**Grounded in research:** cites Zhang et al. (2023) on the link between waiting time and patient satisfaction, and WHO guidance on quality health services.
</details>
